Cincinnati Siding: What the Job Covers

The median Cincinnati home dates to 1951, per the Census ACS. Post-war houses were usually sided in wood lap or cement-asbestos shingle, and a lot of them got an aluminum or vinyl layer in the ’70s or ’90s. A re-side there starts with finding out how many layers you have.

✓Historic approval first. Inside the historic district, the approval comes before the material order (see below).
✓Tear-off and disposal. Cement-asbestos shingle under an aluminum or vinyl layer is a real possibility here. It is never broken up; it stays put or a licensed abatement contractor removes it.
✓Sheathing check. Houses this age were usually sheathed in boards, and some spots never were. Anything soft gets replaced before new wrap goes over it.
✓Material to suit the house. Fiber cement or LP SmartSide lap reads closest to the original wood; vinyl is the budget option.
✓House wrap and window flashing. Wrap lapped shingle-style, with taped flashing at every window and door.
✓Trim, corners and J-channel. Replaced with the siding, so old wood trim doesn’t outlive its welcome behind new walls.
✓Cleanup and a final walkthrough. Nails swept, debris hauled, and we walk every wall together.

⛈️ Cincinnati’s storm record since 2019

NOAA’s Storm Events Database has 75 reports of damaging wind, 1-inch-plus hail or tornadoes within about 7.5 miles of Cincinnati from January 2019 through June 2026. The 3 strongest:

  • April 2, 2025: 63-knot (72 mph) gust measured at Lunken Airport (NOAA)
  • July 2, 2023: 1.25-inch (half-dollar-size) hail at Cincinnati’s East End (NOAA)
  • April 8, 2020: EF0 tornado, 6.2-mile path from Bevis to Hartwell; NOAA damage estimate $100,000 (NOAA)

Hail an inch or larger was reported on 7 days within about 7.5 miles; the largest was 1.25 inches near Cincinnati’s East End on July 2, 2023. On siding, wind is what I see most: panels that came unlocked, loose corner posts, J-channel pulled off a window. Hail marks old vinyl and dents aluminum trim.

📋 Siding permits and approvals

The city’s "Do I Need a Permit?" page lists siding replacement among the work that needs no permit on one- to three-family homes. Source: City of Cincinnati Buildings & Inspections.

Historic districts: The Historic Conservation Office reviews exterior work, and the city’s rule is that work exempt from a building permit still needs a Certificate of Appropriateness. Re-siding a house in a local historic district or on a local landmark needs a COA first. Source.

🌳 Trees and moisture

The city's 2011–2020 canopy assessment put tree cover at 43.1%, ranging from 10% to 70% depending on the neighborhood. On the leafy end of that range, valleys and gutters load up every fall. Source. Shaded walls stay damp longer, which is where wood trim and fiberboard sheathing fail first.

🪵 Not every failure is a whole-house job

Across Hamilton County's housing eras the thing that has actually failed is often wood — a rotted fascia run, soffit boards gone soft, window head trim that has been drinking water through an open caulk joint. Those are board repairs. I will tell you which one you have before quoting a replacement.

Common questions

Asked in Cincinnati

Do storms near Cincinnati damage siding? ▸
NOAA logged 75 damaging-wind, hail or tornado reports within about 7.5 miles of Cincinnati from 2019 through June 2026. The strongest entry near Cincinnati since 2019 is the EF0 tornado on April 8, 2020. The most recent hail an inch or larger within about 7.5 miles was 1 inch near Covington on April 2, 2024. Hail that size shows up on 7 days since 2019, up to 1.25 inches.
Do I need a permit or approval to re-side a house in Cincinnati? ▸
Usually not a building permit. The city’s "Do I Need a Permit?" page lists siding replacement among the work that needs no permit on one- to three-family homes. Source: City of Cincinnati Buildings & Inspections. The Historic Conservation Office reviews exterior work, and the city’s rule is that work exempt from a building permit still needs a Certificate of Appropriateness. Re-siding a house in a local historic district or on a local landmark needs a COA first.
What kind of siding do Cincinnati houses usually have? ▸
It depends on the era. Half of Cincinnati’s homes were built before 1951, per the Census. Most started with wood lap or cement-asbestos shingle; many now wear aluminum or vinyl over it. I check what’s actually on your walls before quoting anything.
How do I know if I need siding replacement or just repair? ▸
Repair fits damage confined to a few panels or one wall. Replacement makes sense when the siding is brittle or faded throughout, the profile is discontinued, or water has gotten behind it. More on the siding replacement page.
How long does siding replacement take? ▸
Two to four days on a typical house, longer for fiber cement. Walls are closed up at the end of every day.
