Drip edge and starter strip, on every roof I build.

Two small parts at the edge of the roof that are easy to skip. Here's what they do and how they go on.

Drip edge is the bent metal strip along the bottom edge and the sloped sides of your roof. It carries water off the edge and into the gutter instead of behind it. Starter strip is the row under the first course of shingles that seals that course down at the edge. Both go on every roof I build, at every eave and rake, and both are part of the roof price.

Why I install it

Why are these on every roof?

The edge of a roof takes the wind first and carries all the water last. Wind gets under the first course of shingles at the eaves and up the sloped sides. Water running off the roof wants to curl back under the edge and down the fascia board behind your gutter. Drip edge and starter strip are the two parts that deal with that.

Without drip edge, water that should drop into the gutter can run down the face of the fascia and soak the wood behind it. On a second-opinion inspection in Bethel this year, the drip edge was missing and the deck edge sat exposed over the gutter.

Starter strip is what the shingle makers build their wind ratings on. TAMKO's 160 mph rating on Titan XT and StormFighter Flex needs TAMKO starter, and GAF's wind coverage on Timberline is written around GAF starter strip. On those roofs I use the maker's own.

— Joe

How it goes on

How the edge goes together.

Order matters here. Get it backwards and the drip edge sends water under the underlayment instead of over it.

01

Eave: drip edge first

Nailed to the deck at the bottom edge and turned down over the fascia before anything else. Ice and water shield laps over it, so water running down the membrane lands on the metal.

02

Rake: drip edge on top

On the sloped sides it's the other way around. The underlayment runs out to the edge of the deck first, then the drip edge goes on over it.

03

Starter: eave and rake

Set a little past the drip edge, a quarter to three quarters of an inch, with its sealant row near the edge. The first course of shingles goes flush over it, and the sealant holds that course down.

04

Over the gutter

At the eave the drip edge hangs over into the gutter, so roof water drops in instead of running down behind it.

Drip edge and starter, answered.

Is drip edge required in Ohio?

Ohio's residential code leaves it to the shingle maker, and GAF requires it. Some cities add their own rule, so check with your building department. On my roofs it goes on at every eave and rake either way.

Is drip edge required in Kentucky?

Yes. Kentucky's residential code requires it, and it's on every roof I build in Northern Kentucky.

Does drip edge go over or under the underlayment?

It depends on the edge. At the eave it goes under: drip edge first, then the ice and water shield laps over it. At the rake it goes over: underlayment first, then the drip edge on top.

Do I need drip edge if I have gutters?

Yes. The drip edge is what gets the water into the gutter. It hangs over into it, so water drops in instead of running down the fascia behind the gutter.

What is a starter strip, and can't you just use cut shingles?

It's the row under the first course of shingles, with a sealant line that holds that course down at the edge. The shingle makers' wind ratings are written around their own starter: TAMKO's 160 mph rating on Titan XT and StormFighter Flex needs TAMKO starter, and GAF's wind coverage on Timberline asks for GAF starter strip. So on those roofs I use the maker's starter strip.

Do drip edge and starter cost extra?

Not on a roof replacement with me. They're part of every roof I build, so they're in the roof price I quote. See how my roof tiers compare.
