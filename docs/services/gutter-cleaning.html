Gutter Cleaning

Cleared By Hand.
Tested, Not Assumed.

Most gutter cleaning calls I get come after somebody notices water pouring over the front edge during a hard rain, or a downspout that just isn't draining like it used to. The fix is straightforward, but doing it right takes actually clearing every run by hand — not blowing the loose stuff off the top and calling it done, which leaves the packed, decomposed debris sitting in the bottom of the trough exactly where it clogs the next downspout.

Every downspout gets a water flush-test once the run is clear, so I know for certain it drains free before I leave — not just that the trough looks empty from the ladder. You get a before/after photo report so you can see the work without climbing up yourself.

If I find something beyond ordinary debris while I'm up there — a sagging section, a loose fastener, a crushed or disconnected downspout — I show you and price it separately before doing anything about it. A routine cleaning call never turns into a bigger job without you seeing it and signing off first.

  • Full interior debris removal, by hand — not just a blower
  • Downspout flush-test on every run to confirm free flow
  • Spot check for granule buildup, staining, and loose or sagging sections
  • Before/after photo report
  • Any damage found flagged and priced separately — never assumed into the cleaning cost
  • Same-day or next-day scheduling often available for routine cleaning
  • One-time visit or standing seasonal service — your call
  • Gutter screen quotes available separately if you'd rather cut down on how often this is needed

Questions & Answers

Gutter Cleaning FAQ

How often do gutters actually need cleaning?
Twice a year is the standard — once after the trees drop leaves in fall, once in spring after the seed pods and helicopters come down. Heavy tree cover overhead can push that to three times a year. I'll tell you honestly what your specific setup needs, not just recommend the maximum.
Do you just blow it out with a leaf blower?
No — every run gets cleared by hand, not just blown around. A blower moves the loose stuff on top and leaves the packed, decomposed debris sitting in the bottom of the trough, which is exactly what clogs a downspout later. Hand-clearing gets all of it, not just what's visible from the ladder.
How do I know it's actually done and not just glanced at?
Every downspout gets flush-tested with water to confirm it drains free, and you get a before/after photo report when I'm done. You don't have to climb a ladder yourself to check the work.
Would gutter screens be a better option than ongoing cleaning?
Fair question, and it depends on your tree cover. Screens cut down how often you need a cleaning, but they don't eliminate it — fine grit and seed pods still get through most screen types over time. I can quote screens separately if that's the direction you want to go; I won't push a bigger job onto a routine cleaning call.
What if you find damage while you're up there?
I'll show you and price it separately before doing anything about it — a sagging section, a loose fastener, a crushed downspout, whatever it is. It never gets assumed into the cleaning price without you seeing it first.
How fast can you get out here?
Routine cleaning usually doesn't need a separate inspection visit first, so it often goes straight on the schedule — same-day or next-day in a lot of cases, depending on the week.
