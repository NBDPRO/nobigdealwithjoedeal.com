Storm Damage in Cincinnati

⛈️ Why Cincinnati roofs are at risk

NOAA logged 75 reports of damaging wind, 1-inch-plus hail or tornadoes within about 7.5 miles of the city's center from 2019 through June 2026. The Ohio Valley's warm, moist air collides with cold fronts rolling off the Great Lakes, producing the kind of severe thunderstorms that shred aging shingles.

Public data, local roofs

Cincinnati Roofs, By the Numbers

The Census median build year for homes in Cincinnati is 1951 (ACS 2020–2024), so half the houses here are older than that. That’s post-war housing: board or early plywood decking, small attic vents, and roofs replaced two or three times, not always with a full tear-off. Only 39.8% of homes are owner-occupied, so plenty of roofs here are a landlord’s call, and those tend to get patched longer than they should.

Town-specific answers

Cincinnati Roofing Questions

Does a roof replacement in Cincinnati need a permit?

Not for the roof covering itself. The city's "Do I Need a Permit?" page exempts roof coverings on one- to three-family homes, as long as there are no more than two layers. A third layer, or structural work on rafters or framing, needs a permit. Historic districts have their own review; see below. Source: City of Cincinnati Buildings & Inspections.

When did hail last hit near Cincinnati?

The most recent report was 1-inch hail on April 2, 2024 near Covington. NOAA logged 9 reports of hail an inch or larger within about 7.5 miles of Cincinnati from 2019 through June 2026, on 7 days. The largest measured 1.25 inches (half-dollar size), on July 2, 2023 near Cincinnati’s East End. Whether your roof took any of it is a separate question, and only an inspection answers it.

How old are most roofs in Cincinnati?

The median Cincinnati home was built in 1951, per the Census Bureau’s 2020–2024 survey, so half the houses are more than 75 years old. By now most have had two or three roofs, and many still sit on the original board decking. If you know when the last tear-off happened, that date matters more than the build year.

Are there historic-district roof rules in Cincinnati?

Yes, in parts of town. In a local historic district or on a local landmark, a roof replacement needs a Certificate of Appropriateness before it starts, even though it needs no building permit. The city's rule: exempt work still requires a COA. The Urban Conservator can approve minor changes; major ones go to the Historic Conservation Board. Outside the district boundaries, the normal permit rules apply.

Which Cincinnati ZIP codes do you cover?

All of them. Most of Cincinnati’s land falls in ZIPs 45230, 45226, 45211, 45223, 45208, 45232, 45224, 45238, 45237, 45225, 45220, 45204, 45205, 45229, 45214, 45202 and 45227, among others, and I work every street.
